Timegrapher 1.0 measures a mechanical watch from the sound of its beats. It measures every beat and keeps the whole record of a take. It states what it measured, with no grades or verdicts: reading the figures is up to you.

Download the file for your computer below. The steps for opening it the first time are in [Installing Timegrapher](https://github.com/peterhudson/timegrapher/blob/main/docs/reference/install.md). The builds are not signed, so macOS and Windows warn you once for each new version.

## What's in it

- **Quick Listen:** put the watch on the microphone and press one button. You get rate, amplitude and beat error, the paper strip, and the Tick Tock profile.
- **Watches:** define a watch once, then keep every take of it in one history.
- **Positions Test:** a guided test in all six positions, and the same test at full wind and 24 hours later, side by side.
- **Power Reserve Test:** how long a full wind lasts, read hour by hour.
- **Steadiness:** nine views of how the readings change over a long take, from Readings Over Time to the FFT and Wavelet maps.
- **Isochronism:** the rate at each amplitude.
- **A command line tool** for recordings and scripts, and a **Claude Desktop extension** that lets an AI assistant run the same measurements.

Every button, pane and reading is explained in the User Guide: what it does, why you would use it, and how each number is made. It is also [on GitHub](https://github.com/peterhudson/timegrapher/blob/main/docs/user-guide/README.md).

## What you need

- A contact microphone in a watch holder. An ordinary microphone often hears the loud drop but misses the quiet unlock, so rate works but amplitude doesn't.
- A mechanical watch with a Swiss lever escapement.
- Its calibre, if you know it. The calibre gives the lift angle, which amplitude needs.

## Telling us what you found

Use **Help > Report a Problem…** in the app to make a zip of what went wrong, or [open an issue](https://github.com/peterhudson/timegrapher/issues/new/choose). Write down what you were doing, what you expected and what happened. A screenshot or the saved take folder helps a lot.
